## summary:
The mechanism responsible for the high-energy emission observed in kiloparsec-scale jets across cosmic time remains poorly understood. The relativistic jet associated with the z=6.1 AGN PSO J030947.49+271757.31 is the highest-redshift large-scale jet currently resolved in X-rays, providing a unique opportunity to study these structures.  We present new Chandra observations, combined with archival data, for a total exposure of 360 ks. The increased exposure allowed us to resolve the extended X-ray emission into two distinct, slightly resolved components and perform spatially resolved spectral analyses.  The extended emission has a photon index of Gamma = 2.25 (+0.45, -0.44), steeper than that of the core (Gamma = 1.86 +/- 0.09), and an extended-to-core luminosity ratio of 18%. Simulations using the Chandra image as input show that an AXIS-like mission could increase the detected photon statistics by a factor of approximately 17.  We resolve the kiloparsec-scale jet into two main components and constrain its spectral properties. This is the most distant and one of the most X-ray-luminous large-scale jets currently known. Future X-ray missions with arcsecond-scale angular resolution could enable more detailed studies of such systems and reveal a population of fainter high-redshift jets beyond Chandra's reach.
