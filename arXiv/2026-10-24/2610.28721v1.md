# Quasinormal-mode stability bounds in gravitational EFT: resolvability, predictivity, and positivity

## authors:
#Leonardo_Juliano
#Enrico_Trincherini

## summary:
It has been proposed that consistency limits how slowly black holes can ring down in extensions of General Relativity: effective-field-theory (EFT) corrections may lengthen the decay time of a quasinormal mode by at most about one oscillation period, $\Re[ω_{\rm GR}]\,δτ\lesssim1$. We argue that, under standard power counting, this condition yields no new, model-independent constraint on gravitational EFTs. First, its motivation does not extend straightforwardly to black holes: the inverse oscillation frequency is not a universal, detector-independent uncertainty in the damping time of a classical waveform. Our main argument, however, rests on eikonal power counting alone. The decay-time shift grows with the angular momentum $\ell$ of the mode, but so does the weight of higher-derivative operators. When curvature and derivatives are controlled by a single scale $Λ$, as in every known ultraviolet completion, the EFT around a black hole of radius $r_s$ remains predictive up to $\ell\sim(r_sΛ)^2$, and the shift becomes of order one only there, where an infinite tower of operators contributes at the same order. A resolvable shift within the predictive regime requires either large numerical coefficients or a two-scale power counting, which has no known ultraviolet completion. In both cases the quartic-curvature operators dominate, and the condition coincides parametrically with the positivity bounds that flat-space graviton amplitudes impose under standard assumptions on the ultraviolet completion. Only in scalar--tensor theories could the two-scale window probe beyond positivity, and then at most one family of dimension-ten operators. The axial sector of Einstein--dilaton--Gauss--Bonnet gravity and Einstein--Maxwell theory illustrate these results.
