# $B_{sJ}(6114)$ as a Higher-Partial-Wave $\bar{K}^{*}B^{(*)}$ Molecular Candidate

## authors:
#Shiquan_Zhang
#Dan_Jiang
#Yin_Huang

## summary:
In 2021, the LHCb Collaboration observed the $B_{sJ}(6114)$ state in the $B^\pm K^\mp$ invariant mass spectrum. It is currently the highest-mass bottom-strange state listed by the Particle Data Group (PDG). However, its spin-parity quantum numbers have not yet been experimentally determined, and its internal structure remains under debate. Although several quark-model interpretations have been proposed, including the $1^3D_1$ state and a member of the $1D(2^-,3^-)$ doublet, the possibility of a hadronic molecular structure for this state has not yet been systematically investigated. We investigate the possibility of interpreting \(B_{sJ}(6114)\) as a \(B^{(*)}\bar{K}^{*}\) molecular state within the one-boson-exchange model. By solving the Schrödinger equation using the Gaussian expansion method, we study the bound-state properties with different spin-parity assignments, taking into account partial-wave mixing and coupled-channel effects. We further calculate the strong decay widths into the allowed \(B^{(*)}\bar{K}\) channels and compare them with the experimental data. Our results show that the mass spectrum alone cannot uniquely determine the spin-parity of \(B_{sJ}(6114)\). However, the decay-width analysis disfavors the \(J^P=0^+\), \(0^-\), \(1^+\), \(2^+\), and \(3^-\) assignments, while the \(J^P=1^-\), \(2^-\), and \(3^+\) configurations remain compatible with the experimental constraints. Among these possibilities, the \(J^P=1^-\) assignment provides the best agreement with the measured decay width. These findings suggest that \(B_{sJ}(6114)\) may be a higher-partial-wave \(B^{(*)}\bar{K}^{*}\) molecular state.
