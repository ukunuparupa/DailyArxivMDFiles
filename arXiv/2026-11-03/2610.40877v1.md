# A systematic study of hydrodynamic attractors in periodically driven systems at weak and strong couplings

## authors:
#Aleksas_Mazeliauskas
#Toshali_Mitra
#Sören_Schlichting
#Simon_Schneider
#Louis_Nwakile_Onwuka
#Martin_Vrdoljak

## summary:
We study equilibration and hydrodynamic response in conformal systems undergoing periodic expansion and contraction along one spatial direction. Using strongly coupled holography (AdS/CFT), weakly coupled kinetic theories (relaxation-time approximation, scalar $φ^4$ theory and the Yang--Mills sector of QCD effective kinetic theory) and hydrodynamics (Navier--Stokes, Müller--Israel--Stewart (MIS) and rBRSSS), we investigate the shear response to a periodic drive of varying amplitude $A$ and frequency $ω$, both analytically in linear response and numerically. We find that, at the linear level, $A\ll 1$, periodically driven systems approach a cyclic attractor that in general differs from Navier--Stokes behaviour. This attractor is universal among kinetic theories and accurately described by MIS theory: for slow driving, $ωτ_π\ll 1$, where $τ_π$ is the shear relaxation time, it approaches the Navier--Stokes limit, whereas for fast driving the shear stress saturates at its non-interacting (free-streaming) value. In strongly coupled holographic systems, the periodic drive excites non-hydrodynamic quasinormal modes, which produce a characteristic ringing in the approach to the cyclic attractor, in contrast to the purely exponential approach found in kinetic theories. This difference is a direct consequence of the distinct non-hydrodynamic spectra of the two classes of theories. Moreover, unlike in kinetic theory, the holographic response is sensitive to the curvature terms of second-order hydrodynamics. Over longer times, non-linear viscous heating, which is quadratic in the drive amplitude, raises the temperature and thereby lowers $ωτ_π$, since $τ_π\sim 1/T$. This induces a slow drift of the cyclic attractor, which eventually converges towards Navier--Stokes hydrodynamics at asymptotically late times.
