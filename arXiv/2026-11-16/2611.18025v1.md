# Second order and secondary functional renormalization group flow equations

## authors:
#Stefan_Floerchinger

## summary:
The functional renormalization group provides a powerful non-perturbative framework for quantum field theory through the exact flow equation for the scale-dependent effective action. A mass-type regulator $R_k = k^2$ is particularly attractive because it preserves symmetries, locality, and unitarity (or Osterwalder--Schrader reflection positivity in the Euclidean domain). However, unlike momentum-dependent regulators, it does not tame all ultraviolet divergences: in four dimensions, flow equations for the effective potential exhibit logarithmic divergences. Motivated by Callan's perturbative renormalization scheme, where higher-order $θ$-insertions improve ultraviolet convergence, we develop two classes of higher-order functional flow equations. First, we derive exact second-order flow equations by taking further derivatives of the standard flow equation with respect to $k$. These have either a one-loop structure involving the first derivative of the inverse propagator, or a mixed one-loop and two-loop structure. While exact and useful for incorporating two-loop effects and testing truncation schemes, they do not in general resolve the ultraviolet convergence problem. Second, we introduce a secondary flow method, where variations of an effective mass-squared parameter introduce an additional flow, with parameter $l$. The resulting secondary flow equations become ultraviolet finite once sufficiently many derivatives have been taken, and can be evaluated without an additional ultraviolet regulator. Integrating back to the standard flow requires integration constants that encode microscopic ultraviolet physics, analogous to relevant coupling constants.
