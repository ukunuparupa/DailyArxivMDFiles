# Baryon and lepton asymmetries across the cosmic QCD transition: effects of a 3D first-order surface

## summary:
We investigate the cosmological QCD phase transition by calculating the cosmic trajectories from the five equations that impose the conservation of the baryon and lepton asymmetries, the charge neutrality of the Universe, and $β$ equilibrium. We perform the calculation using for the first time the four-dimensional $(T, μ_B, μ_Q, μ_S)$ Constant Entropy Contour equation of state, which for $μ_S = 0$ ($β$-equilibrium) features a critical line and a first-order phase transition (FOPT) surface in the three-dimensional $(T, μ_B, μ_Q)$ space. We first show that, within the standard cosmological scenario with baryon asymmetry $b_{\text{obs}}=8.65\cdot 10^{-11}$, the lepton asymmetries alone can never induce a FOPT. We then explore the non-standard cosmological scenario with large values of $b$ and find that reaching a FOPT requires $b\gtrsim0.028$ during the QCD epoch. Such a value is compatible with observations only if the baryon asymmetry is diluted to $b_{\text{obs}}$ after the transition, which a period of little inflation would provide with $N\approx6.5$ $e$-folds of expansion. Finally, we study the phenomenological imprints on the equation of state and primordial black hole (PBH) formation, showing that pion condensation at large lepton asymmetries drives $c_s^2 > 1/3$ and shifts the PBH peak to sub-solar masses ($0.3-0.8\,M_\odot$), while the large-$b$ FOPT yields a narrow spike around $4.2\,M_\odot$.
