# Classical weak antilocalization in electron hydrodynamics

## authors:
#Alberto_Cortijo

## summary:
Weak localization is strongly suppressed in electron hydrodynamics, where frequent electron--electron collisions rapidly dephase the Cooperon. Nevertheless, disorder can still produce a logarithmic correction to the bare hydrodynamic resistivity. We show that spatial fluctuations of the momentum-relaxation rate generate a classical memory kernel through the diffusive return of transverse momentum. In two dimensions, this return is logarithmic, as in weak localization, but involves a single shear propagator rather than quantum interference. Conservation laws and positivity of dissipation fix its antilocalizing sign: the return reduces momentum relaxation and hence the bulk resistivity. Unlike quantum weak localization, the logarithm is cut off by momentum relaxation and has a viscosity-dependent amplitude. For temperature-independent impurity friction, the resistivity correction scales as $-T^2\ln(T_ξ/T)$ in a Fermi liquid and $-T\ln(T_ξ/T)$ in a Planckian metal. In channels, the return competes with viscosity renormalization, and a width scan separates the two effects.
