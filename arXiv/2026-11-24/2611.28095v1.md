# Horizon mergers and initial test-charge acceleration in charged black-hole geometries with anisotropic sources

## authors:
#Saeed_Noori_Gashti
#Mohammad_Ali_S_Afshar
#Mohammad_Reza_Alipour

## summary:
We study horizon mergers and the initial radial acceleration of charged test particles in static, spherically symmetric black-hole geometries with a string cloud, a Kiselev power-law component, or a logarithmic profile conventionally. After separating the Maxwell contribution, the effective environmental stress tensors are anisotropic and are not identified with homogeneous quintessence or pressureless cold dark matter. Using a common mass-function formulation, we derive the horizon-merger conditions and classify the Kiselev branches. In this family, the inner-event merger charge provides only an upper bound: three distinct positive horizons can additionally require a positive lower charge bound associated with the event-cosmological merger. For a test particle released from rest relative to a static observer, we derive the local charge-to-mass threshold for outward initial acceleration. Writing $λ=q/μ$ and $z=Q/Q_{\rm ext}$, where $Q_{\rm ext}$ is the inner-event merger charge, the subextremal string-cloud threshold has infimum $1/z$, approached only at infinity. For the subextremal logarithmic profile, the threshold exceeds unity throughout the finite exterior and attains a finite global minimum. For $α/M=0.4$ and $Q/M=1.2$, this minimum is $λ_*\simeq1.251125$. In a nondegenerate Kiselev static patch, the neutral balance radius is unique and radially unstable. Density matching supplies conditional profile normalizations, not observational fits. For the logarithmic profile, the integration parameter is distinguished from the mass combination inferred from circular motion. These results characterize geometric horizon bounds and test-particle dynamics; they do not establish a two-black-hole force law or a mechanism for cosmic acceleration, nor do they constitute a test of the Weak Gravity Conjecture.
