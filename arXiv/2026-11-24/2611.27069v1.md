# Beyond the Peak: Constraining early dark energy with the damping tail

## authors:
#Hao_Wang
#Gen_Ye
#Yun-Song_Piao

## summary:
Early dark energy is commonly characterized by the amplitude and location of its primary energy-injection peak. We show that its post-peak evolution, which we call the \emph{EDE tail}, provides an additional observable probe of the underlying scalar dynamics and can materially affect its ability to alleviate the Hubble tension. We construct a simple phenomenological framework in which distinct regions of a multiscale scalar potential govern the dynamics at different field scales. As a representative realization, we consider a potential that combines axion-like and $α$-attractor-like regimes, thereby allowing the EDE tail to be reshaped through reflection and asymmetric oscillations. Confronting this framework with cosmic microwave background, baryon acoustic oscillation, and supernova observations, we find an improved fit to the high-$\ell$ CMB data together with a higher inferred bestfit $H_0$. Our results show that the EDE tail retains observable information about the underlying scalar potential that is not captured by the conventional peak parameters alone. This provides a complementary route for constructing and observationally distinguishing EDE scenarios, and potentially for further alleviating the Hubble tension.
