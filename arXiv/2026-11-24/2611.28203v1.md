# Level repulsion and the quantum viscosity of near-extremal black branes

## authors:
#Panos_Betzios
#Olga_Papadoulaki
#Maria_Myrto_Pegioudi

## summary:
Below the gap scale of the AdS$_2$ throat, quantum fluctuations make the shear viscosity of near-extremal black branes grow as $(CT)^{-1/2}$ in the disk approximation of JT gravity, and the one-loop $η/s$ diverges. To cure this divergence, we include the non-perturbative completion of JT gravity as a matrix integral. The Kubo formula then probes universal features of level correlations. At finite extremal entropy $S_0$, level repulsion makes the DC viscosity vanish. A finite, universal viscosity survives in a double-scaling limit in which the probe frequency $ω$ is held in a fixed ratio to the level-spacing scale $ω_{\rm sp}\equiv e^{-S_0}/C$. At fixed $ω$, with $ω_{\rm sp}\llω\ll e^{S_0/3}ω_{\rm sp}$ the Schwarzian growth then saturates at $η_{\max}\simeq0.174\,(ω/ω_{\rm sp})\,η_{sc}$, where $η_{sc}$ is the semiclassical viscosity, and the viscosity decreases at lower temperatures. At the extremely low temperature scale $CT\sim e^{-2S_0/3}$ the genus expansion and the one-loop entropy computations completely break down. There we start probing the edge of the spectrum and the problem reduces exactly to the Airy kernel. We find that the ratio of the quenched averages of $η$ and $s$ is finite and positive at every non-zero temperature. We compare our results with explicit completions defined by the string equation, in which the spectrum is bounded from below. The bulk results are the same in these completions and the quenched edge results change only by a few per cent, while the annealed quantities depend on the non-perturbative completion at order one.
