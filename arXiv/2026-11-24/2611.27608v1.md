# Effective theory of quantum black holes with Kretschmann scalar

## authors:
#G_I_B_Darman
#B_N_Jayawiguna
#M_F_Fauzi
#A_Sulaksono

## summary:
We investigate the thermodynamics, quasinormal modes (QNMs), and shadows of the effective theory of quantum black holes (BHs) by expanding the physical constants in terms of the Kretschmann scalar. The resulting effective metric can contain two horizons, which is typical for a quantum-corrected Schwarzschild spacetime. The second-order corrected metric only shifts the first-order corrected metric, allowing investigation of IR phenomena from the pure first-order corrected one. From a thermodynamic point of view, the metric leads to a stable cold remnant with zero entropy, compatible with the third law of thermodynamics. In the perturbation regime, we use massless scalar perturbations to extract the effective potential, QNMs, graybody factor, and total absorption cross section. We show that double-horizon BHs have a double light ring in the potential, and for a certain value $Ω/χ^2=0.01,$ the QNMs are similar to the GR case since the horizon is Schwarzschild-like. We also tested the correspondence between the QNMs and the graybody factor, and our results still hold with an error far below $1\%$. Our results also conclude that the higher the frequency, the bigger the cross-section area. For the optical observables, we observe that the opposite sign of $Ω$ leads to distinct behavior of the BH's photon sphere and shadow radius. The Sagittarius A* shadow radius upper bound from the Event Horizon Telescope observation in the $2σ$ and $1σ$ significance set the upper limit of the quantum parameter $Ω$ to $Ω/χ^2 \lesssim 0.3649$ and $Ω/χ^2 \lesssim 0.0105$, respectively.
