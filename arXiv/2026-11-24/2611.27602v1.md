# Probing grand unification in the dark dimension: proton decay and collider signatures

## authors:
#Waqas_Ahmed
#George_K_Leontaris

## summary:
We study the geometric connection between a stretched grand-unified state, proton decay, and electroweak Kaluza--Klein (KK) resonances in a dark-dimension-inspired interval model. The gravitational length sets the five-dimensional Planck scale, while a shorter gauge interval controls the KK spectrum and the stretched-state mass. We construct normalized fermion and Higgs profiles, diagonalize the neutral-vector sector, and determine its fermionic widths. Proton partial lifetimes are evaluated using a two-operator, SU(5)-inspired effective matching prescription. One-loop four-dimensional MSSM and split-supersymmetric reference running determines electroweak coupling-crossing scales and the residual strong-coupling mismatch. A selected common-threshold benchmark gives a heavy-state mass of $1.137\times10^{16}$ GeV, a reference crossing of $1.139\times10^{16}$ GeV, and a conditional proton partial lifetime of $8.72\times10^{35}$ years. At a micron-scale gravitational length, suppressed baryon-violating matching amplitudes can satisfy proton limits, although the high-scale mass-matching condition selects a distinct region of the scanned geometry. Independently, a $5$ TeV neutral-vector benchmark yields an inclusive leading-order dielectron cross section of $0.183$ fb over $4.5$--$5.5$ TeV at a collider energy of $13.6$ TeV, before detector selection. The analysis provides quantitative effective-model benchmarks and identifies the remaining high-scale matching and experimental tests.
