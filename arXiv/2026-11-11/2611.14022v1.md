# Tunneling from an oscillating state in quantum field theory

## authors:
#Oliver_Janssen
#Matthew_Kleban
#Mehrdad_Mirbabayi
#Cameron_Norton

## summary:
How does a scalar field oscillating about a metastable minimum decay? For a coherent state with $\langle q(t) \rangle = a \cos mt$ we find two results. When $mR \ll 1$, with $m$ the mass and $R$ the bubble radius, the decay at time $t$ is a Coleman bubble in the frozen potential $U_t(η) = U(\langle q \rangle +η)-U(\langle q \rangle)-U'(\langle q \rangle)η$, for any amplitude. At small amplitude but any $mR$, $B(t)=B_0 - a \mathcal{M} \cos mt+O(a^2)$, with $\mathcal{M}$ set by the tail of the vacuum bounce; for a thin wall $\mathcal{M} \propto e^{mR}$, so exponentially small oscillations change the rate significantly. Both results follow from a Euclidean source construction. The same method computes the tunneling rate of a field slowly rolling in one direction and metastable in another.
