# The first-order phase transitions and gravitational waves from an effective theory inspired by four-stack intersecting D6-brane models

## authors:
#Haotian_Huangfu
#Tianjun_Li
#Qi_Sun
#Rui_Sun
#Wenxing_Zhang

## summary:
First-order phase transitions in the early Universe can generate stochastic gravitational-wave backgrounds in frequency bands targeted by proposed deci-Hz observatories. We study the gravitational-wave consequences of the right-handed gauge symmetry breaking $SU(2)_{R_1}\times SU(2)_{R_2}\to SU(2)_R$ in a Pati-Salam effective theory inspired by four-stack intersecting D6-brane models. The ultraviolet construction fixes the gauge structure and particle multiplicities, while the scalar masses and quartic couplings are treated as renormalized inputs below the supersymmetry-breaking scale. Using a one-loop finite-temperature potential with ring resummation, we follow a strongly supercooled transition at the few-TeV scale through nucleation, percolation, completion, and reheating. The leading-order friction diagnostic does not support a runaway wall, so sound waves dominate the gravitational-wave signal, with turbulence as a sub-leading contribution; the finite acoustic lifetime suppresses the peak. For the fast-wall reference case, the spectrum peaks at $f_{\rm peak}=0.611$ Hz with $h^2Ω_{\rm GW,peak}=1.232\times10^{-11}$, in the deci-Hz band targeted by DECIGO and BBO. We present its dependence on the wall speed, renormalization scale, and thermal screening, together with a noise-only comparison for several proposed detector configurations.
