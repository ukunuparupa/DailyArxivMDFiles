# Zeta function in Coulomb background

## authors:
#M_Bordag
#I_G_Pirozhenko

## summary:
We calculate the one-loop vacuum energy of a massive scalar field confined to the interior of a sphere with Dirichlet boundary conditions and subject to a Coulomb background potential $α/r$. The spectrum is determined by the zeros of a confluent hypergeometric (Kummer) function, and the corresponding zeta function is constructed and analytically continued to $s=-1/2$. The renormalization is performed by subtracting the divergent part of the zeta function, defined through the heat kernel expansion, which removes the non-decreasing powers of the mass and leaves a finite, physically normalized vacuum energy. While the heat kernel coefficients for the pure Bessel (Coulomb-free) case are well known, the Coulomb contributions to the coefficients are not; we determine them by matching the analytic continuation of the zeta function to the general form of the divergent part, obtaining terms proportional to $α$ and $α^2$. A key technical point is that the uniform asymptotic expansion of the mode generating function cannot be used in full; a part must be retained unexpanded, and an integral representation of the digamma function is used to carry out the analytic continuation. In the infinite-volume limit the renormalized vacuum energy tends to a constant $E_0^{ren,\infty}(α,m)=αe(α/m)$, with a dimensionless function $e$ that depends only on the ratio $\al/m$. This limit shows that a finite, physically meaningful renormalized vacuum energy can be defined for a massive scalar field in the background of a Coulomb source. For $m\to\infty$ it vanishes in accordance with the normalization condition, while for $m\to0$ it diverges, so that the present calculation does not apply to a massless field, pointing to the need of another vacuum state in that case.
