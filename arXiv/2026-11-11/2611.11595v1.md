# A geometrical approach for obtaining exact analytical solutions for a scalar field in a one-dimensional dynamical cavity

## summary:
The dynamical Casimir effect was first predicted by G. T. Moore in 1969, who considered a real scalar field inside a cavity with a moving mirror in ($1+1$)D, satisfying Dirichlet boundary conditions. Moore wrote the general solution for the field in terms of a function that satisfies a functional equation nowadays known as the Moore equation.In the literature, exact analytical solutions to the Moore equation form a rather small set, restricted to specific classes of laws of motion. Cole and Schieve (1995) developed a geometrical approach that yielded a general formula for the solution of the Moore equation for cavities initially at rest. However, its application has always been done through numerical calculations, and their approach has therefore long been regarded solely as a numerical method. In this work, we show that their method can also be employed as a powerful method for deriving exact analytical solutions, and show the applicability of this approach to deriving analytical solutions to the Moore equation. We obtain original exact analytical solutions for an expanding cavity with a uniformly moving mirror, a contracting cavity with a mirror that approaches the static one asymptotically, and, in addition, we recover the first analytical solution to the Moore equation for a resonant mirror motion, originally obtained by Law in 1994. Moreover, we extend the Cole-Schieve approach, showing that exact solutions can be constructed from any spacetime region where the Moore function has already been determined. We show that this extended geometrical approach allows us to expand the set of known analytical solutions to the Moore equation from the few classes of motion reported in the literature to an infinite set, as it enables the treatment of new classes of laws of motion obtained by combining those presented here with each other as well as with previously known classes of trajectories.
