# Landau-Ginzburg Theory of a Holographic Superconductor: A Horizon Perspective on the Zero-Temperature Ground State

## authors:
#Sejin_Kim
#Yunseok_Seo

## summary:
We investigate the effect of a quartic scalar self-interaction, $λ|φ|^4$, on a holographic superconductor in which non-linear electrodynamic couplings generate a superconducting dome containing an extended Lifshitz regime. The quartic interaction leaves the linearized scalar dynamics around the normal phase unchanged while modifying the non-linear solutions where the scalar field is large. As $λ$ increases, the solutions within the dome pass through three stages. The Lifshitz region shrinks until its scaling solution disappears at $λ^*$, leaving a no-AdS region, beyond whose edge the flows end before reaching any asymptotic region, and above $λ_c$ the dome consists entirely of asymptotically AdS hairy black holes. At the bottom of the dome we identify the point where the horizon value of the scalar field is largest, which we refer to as the critical point. There this value lies at the deepest symmetry-breaking minimum that the horizon effective potential, of Landau-Ginzburg form, can have. Unlike a conventional Landau-Ginzburg description, this interpretation rests on the scalar field at the horizon rather than on the boundary order parameter. Below $λ^*$, the constant scalar of the Lifshitz fixed point sits instead at the minimum of the effective potential of its asymptotic scaling region.
