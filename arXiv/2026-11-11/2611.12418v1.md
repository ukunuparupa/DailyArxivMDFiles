# First-order origins of second-order supersymmetry

## authors:
#Stevan_Cebrian
#Mikhail_S_Plyushchay

## summary:
One-dimensional second-order supersymmetry is constructed from conserved first-order operators on a surface with a cyclic coordinate. In Witten's mechanics with two fermionic modes, a centrally extended $N=4$ algebra permits a conserved product connecting the zero- and two-fermion sectors. Cyclic reduction gives the second-order supercharge and the correlated quantum corrections in the charge and partner Hamiltonians. Isothermal coordinates identify the auxiliary Hill equation with the separated Laplace or wave equation for the gauge-transformed charge kernel, and express the correction as a Schwarzian derivative. The central momentum fixes the quadratic invariant $C$: the Riemannian construction gives $C\leq0$, and its indefinite counterpart gives $C\geq0$, with a separately chosen positive adjoint after reduction. Diagonalizing the one-fermion intermediate recovers real scalar factorizations for $C\leq0$ and complex ones for $C>0$. Pauli reductions provide a second first-order mechanism; on the hyperbolic plane they reveal a curvature-dependent invariant and additional charges of a regular free-particle--Pöschl--Teller pair in a selected momentum sector. In one dimension, a formally Hermitian two-component intermediate with a positive inner product yields local first-order chains for arbitrary $C$. For a constant superpotential, a real transverse deformation on a flat cylinder also gives a first-order surface realization of $C>0$ with a positive inner product.
