# An RG perspective on the (un)naturalness of Starobinsky inflation

## authors:
#Francesca_Campani
#Francesco_Ferrarin
#Serena_Giardino
#Alessia_Platania

## summary:
Starobinsky inflation is often regarded as a successful minimal model of the early universe, yet it relies on a large $R^2$-coupling, in apparent tension with the expectation that Wilson coefficients in a gravitational effective field theory are of the same order. We test this expectation with a first-principles renormalization group calculation. We use asymptotically safe gravity as a testing ground to define ultraviolet-complete renormalization group trajectories and to uniquely predict higher-order couplings in terms of the free parameters of the theory---the cosmological constant and the $R^2$-coupling in Planck units. Including higher-curvature operators up to the fourth order, we find that indeed the flow generates dimensionless couplings of comparable size in the ultraviolet, with the irrelevant couplings tied to the relevant ones by linear relations. However, at sub-Planckian energies a canonical-like scaling takes over, and the irrelevant couplings get suppressed by powers of the ratio between the inflationary and Planck scales. This hierarchy yields the suppression of higher-order operators needed for consistency with cosmological data, so that the $R^2$-dominated dynamics emerges as a natural low-energy approximation. At the same time, the higher-order terms required by fundamental physics induce small but calculable deviations from the Starobinsky predictions, which may be worth considering in light of the latest and future DESI and ACT results.
