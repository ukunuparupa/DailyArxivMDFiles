# Shallow-water black holes and lattice superradiance

## authors:
#Antonino_Flachi

## summary:
Rotating black holes can amplify waves that scatter off them, a process known as superradiance that fluid analogues bring into the laboratory. Separating the horizon's role from dissipation in the surrounding fluid remains difficult. Here we realise a rotating analogue black hole in a lattice fluid: a shallow-water vortex whose horizon is set by a topographic sill, so that the only sink lies behind a supercritical layer that lets nothing out. We measure superradiance as energy extraction: the flux at the horizon is outward for co-rotating waves whose pattern rotates more slowly than the horizon, and inward for faster waves, counter-rotating waves and a non-rotating hole. A scan over three lattice viscosities separates the extraction at the horizon, which is inviscid, from that of the viscous fluid inside the corotation radius: well below the band edge the horizon is the absorber; near the edge the viscous share is half at the operating viscosity and vanishes at the lowest. A drain-anchored horizon, as in water-tank experiments, gives the same signs. Our analysis gives insight into the mechanism of superradiance and offers a cheap, flexible platform for designing analogue-gravity experiments.
