# Strange-Metal Magnetotransport without a Strange-Metal Ground State

## authors:
#Yongjun_Ahn
#Matteo_Baggioli
#Hyun-Sik_Jeong
#Keun-Young_Kim

## summary:
Strange metals display a linear-in-temperature resistivity, $ρ_{xx}\sim T$, and a quadratic inverse Hall angle, $\cotΘ_H\sim T^2$. These seemingly universal laws are often attributed to quantum criticality, yet no controlled theory realizes both at an asymptotic zero-temperature fixed point, and experimentally they persist only over finite temperature windows. Here, we show that these properties can arise from an intermediate critical regime that does not govern the ground state. Using a minimal class of holographic models, we construct a fully backreacted realization of both scalings, which appear within a finite temperature window controlled by an emergent intermediate scaling geometry. The window is bounded by relevant deformations of the corresponding fixed point: momentum relaxation sets the upper edge, while the magnetic field $B$ closes it from below at $T_{\min}\sim B^{2/3}$, up to logarithmic corrections. At $T_{\min}$, strange-metal transport, the scaling geometry, and critical thermodynamics break down together. Our results point to intermediate criticality as a distinct route to strange-metal transport, and suggest that it can be identified by the simultaneous breakdown of transport and thermodynamic scaling under a magnetic field.
