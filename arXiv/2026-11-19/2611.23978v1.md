# Geoneutrinos (and reactor neutrinos) do not visibly decay

## authors:
#Mauricio_Bustamante

## summary:
Neutrinos that decay on their way to a detector arrive depleted, more so at low energies, where time dilation lengthens their lifetime the least. Geoneutrinos---electron antineutrinos from the radioactive decay of uranium and thorium inside the Earth---have energies of a few MeV and travel up to the diameter of the Earth. We use them to test neutrino decay for the first time. We compute the geoneutrino signal at Borexino, KamLAND, SNO+, and JUNO for visible decay, in which the daughter neutrinos are themselves detected, and compare it against their published data. We find no evidence of decay that holds across Earth models. Combining the four experiments, we limit the decay $ν_1 \to ν_3$ in the inverted mass ordering at 90% C.L.: $τ_1/m_1 > 3.7 \times 10^{-9}$ s eV$^{-1}$ when the radiogenic power of the Earth is left free and $τ_1/m_1 > 4.4 \times 10^{-9}$ to $1.6 \times 10^{-8}$ s eV$^{-1}$ when it is taken from Earth models. With an Earth model assumed, the geoneutrinos set the limit; with the power free, the antineutrinos from nuclear reactors that the same experiments detect count as much as the geoneutrinos. These are the first terrestrial limits on the visible decay of $ν_1$.
