# Quantum Monge-Kantorovich optimal transport in spacetime through spatiotemporal Kirkwood-Dirac distributions and spatiotemporal states

## authors:
#Zhian_Jia

## summary:
Monge-Kantorovich optimal transport is a mathematical framework that determines the most efficient way to transform one probability distribution into another while minimizing a given cost function. Its central constructions include the Wasserstein distances, which quantify the space of probability distributions, and dual formulations that link to convex analysis. Its standard quantum generalization replaces probability distributions with density operators and cost function with positive semidefinite cost operator, which should be regarded as spatial quantum optimal transport. In this work, we propose the quantum optimal transport problem in spacetime through the spatiotemporal phase-space Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobability distribution and spatiotemporal states and through spatiotemporal state. We show that the two formalisms are equivalent and related via spatiotemporal operator frames. In the Kirkwood-Dirac phase-space formalism, the spatiotemporal distribution and the cost function are complex-valued, and the spatiotemporal couplings are Kirkwood-Dirac quasiprobability distributions that yield the correct marginals for the local states. In the spatiotemporal state formalism, the cost operators are generally non-Hermitian, and the spatiotemporal couplings are generally non-Hermitian and yield the correct reduced local states. The Margenau-Hill phase-space distribution gives rise to a real-valued optimal transport in spatiotemporal phase space; in the spatiotemporal state formalism, this corresponds to a Hermitian spatiotemporal coupling state. We give a rigorous definitions and a careful discussion of its duality. We also introduce a spatiotemporal quantum Wasserstein metric based on a metric in spatiotemporal phase spaces and show that it satisfies the metric axioms.
