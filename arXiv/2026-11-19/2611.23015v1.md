# A Universal Axion in a Twin-QCD Sector

## authors:
#Stuart_Raby
#Scott_Watson

## summary:
Dark matter may consist of multiple components with distinct particle-physics origins. We investigate a scenario in which a universal axion couples to both visible and twin QCD, with twin baryons providing a possible dominant dark-matter component. The presence of a second strongly interacting sector can substantially enhance the axion mass while preserving its role in resolving the strong-CP problem, provided the physical vacuum angles are appropriately aligned. For a representative uniformly rescaled twin spectrum with a confinement scale five times that of ordinary QCD, the axion mass is enhanced by approximately a factor of 25.  Using lattice-informed QCD results and a two-temperature description of the early universe, we investigate the cosmological evolution and relative abundances of the dark-sector components. In a representative conditional numerical benchmark, axions account for a small percentage of the observed dark matter, while twin baryons could constitute the remainder for a suitable primordial baryon asymmetry. We also examine the implications for additional relativistic species, strong-CP alignment, and the interactions of stable twin matter.  Our results provide a quantitative framework for exploring mixed axion--twin-baryon dark matter and identify the particle-physics and cosmological conditions necessary for a viable realization, including constraints from the thermal history and formation of cosmic structure.
