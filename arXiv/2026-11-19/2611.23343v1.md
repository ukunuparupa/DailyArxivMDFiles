# An energy-dependent rescaling of the neutrino propagation Hamiltonian: Standard Model Extension identification, matter sector, and long-baseline degeneracy

## authors:
#Neda_Razzaghi

## summary:
We study the consequences of multiplying the neutrino propagation Hamiltonian by a real, energy-dependent scalar, $H_η(E)=f(E)H_0(E)$ with $f(E)=1-η_0(E/E_0)^2$, a minimal-length ansatz. Its vacuum part is a dimension-four, CPT-even Lorentz-violating term of the Standard Model Extension with coefficient $(\cL)_{ii}=-η_0(m_i^2-m_1^2)/(2E_0^2)$, diagonal in the mass basis and fixed by the mass matrix, hence removable by a reparametrization. The matter-potential rescaling has dimension-five behaviour and is CPT-odd. For $f=1-η_0(E/E_0)^p$ the vacuum term behaves as a dimension $p+2$ operator and the matter term as $p+3$, so only $p=2$ gives a marginal vacuum operator; $p=0$ is absorbed into the measured mass splittings, and non-integer $p$ admits no local operator. For $η_0=0.02$ at $E_0=2.5$~GeV we find $|(\cL)_{33}|\simeq4\times10^{-24}$, comparable to present atmospheric bounds, whereas a Planck-suppressed deformation with $β_0=\mathcal{O}(1)$ gives $3.2\times10^{-58}$. At constant density it is equivalent to $L\to f(E)L$: mixing angles and the Dirac phase are unchanged, the CP asymmetry is reparametrized rather than generated, and the leading observable is a rescaling of $\dm_{31}$. At $1297$~km a re-fit of $\dm_{31}$ alone absorbs $23$~per cent of the squared probability difference, and a four-parameter re-fit absorbs $41.5$~per cent. No statistical analysis is performed; degeneracy means a distance in probability space, not a confidence interval.
