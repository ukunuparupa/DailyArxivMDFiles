# Thermalization of the Primordial Stochastic Gravitational-Wave Background

## authors:
#Hongguang_Zhang
#Zhengyang_Qiu
#Sirui_Wang
#Jiayi_Wei

## summary:
It is conventionally believed that the Stochastic Gravitational-Wave Background (SGWB) from inflation provides information about the earliest universe. However, in this Letter, we present a different picture. Our analysis starts from the observation that the inflation-predicted spectrum implies gravitons reside in high-occupation states, which significantly enhances their self-interactions and triggers rapid thermalization. We show that approximately $80\%$ of the primordial gravitons participate in this thermalization, forming a Bose--Einstein distribution and thereby erasing the primordial information. Evaluated using typical inflation parameters, this thermalized spectrum yields a present-day peak frequency of $f_{0, \text{peak}}^{(\text{th})} \sim 1.4 \times 10^8 \, \mathrm{Hz}$ in the hundred-megahertz band with a peak amplitude of $Ω_{0, \text{peak}}^{(\text{th})} \sim 3.5 \times 10^{-13}$. Due to the sharp drop-off away from the peak, the resulting spectrum lies beyond the reach of low-frequency observatories like PTAs, LISA, and LIGO, but remains uniquely accessible to high-frequency detectors such as microwave cavity resonators and dielectric haloscopes.
