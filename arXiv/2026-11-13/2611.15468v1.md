# Radiation from scattering on multiple shockwaves

## authors:
#Tim_Adamo
#Anton_Ilderton
#Hannah_Kingham

## summary:
It is well known that radiation produced by scattering an electron off multiple sources depends on the length of the mean free path between these scatterings. When this is sufficiently small, the spectrum is suppressed due to destructive interference, a famous result known as the Landau-Pomeranchuk-Migdal (LPM) effect. This is usually modelled approximately, with scattering centres treated linearly (e.g., as screened Coulomb potentials), high-energy probes and soft emitted photons. In this paper, we consider a scenario where multiple scatterings can be computed exactly: charged probe scattering on multiple null electromagnetic shockwaves. Here, the amplitudes for elastic scattering and single photon emission can be determined exactly, for arbitrary kinematics, in terms of the shock profiles in the transverse plane. We use these amplitudes to compute the radiation density (doubly-averaged over shock locations in the transverse plane and lightfront time) in the regimes of independent and interfering scatterings. For shock profiles which are radially symmetric in the transverse plane (including the ultraboosted Coulomb field), we recover the known LPM suppression, albeit with more general kinematics. In addition, our setup allows us to consider the source-free case of multiple scattering on impulsive/ultra-short plane waves, where we find much stronger suppression of the spectrum in the LPM regime.
