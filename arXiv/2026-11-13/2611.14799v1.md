# Casimir Attraction and Repulsion in a Magnetic Domain-Wall Background

## authors:
#Inés_Cavero-Peláez
#Beatriz_Gómez-López
#Jose_M_Muñoz-Castañeda
#Gonzalo_Sancho-Garrido

## summary:
We study the one-loop Casimir interaction between two $δ$--$δ'$ plates in the fixed one-soliton Pöschl--Teller background of the sine-Gordon kink. The transverse fluctuation problem is the Gaussian truncation of an $S^2$ sigma model, equivalently an easy-axis antiferromagnet with a domain wall, in which the plates exert no classical force on the wall. For $v\ge2|w|$ the fluctuation operators are non-negative, and strictly stable couplings, $v>2|w|$, also exclude zero modes, so the quadratic field theory is consistent at the fixed sine-Gordon mass. The background-dressed relative determinant factorises exactly into effective, gradient-dependent strengths of the two plates, and the sign of each frequency channel of the interaction energy is fixed by their orientation. Two identical, equally oriented, strictly stable plates switch from attraction near the kink to repulsion away from it under a rigid translation at fixed couplings and separation. The interaction energy and the force conjugate to the separation neither share their sign everywhere nor change sign at the same positions: there are regions of negative energy and repulsive force, so attraction and repulsion must be read from the force. For such plates with $w\neq0$ the force is repulsive at short and attractive at large separation, and the interaction energy has a minimum at a separation that depends on the position of the pair. For $v=1.05$, $w=0.5$ and $d=1.5$ the energy changes sign at $c\simeq-0.44$ and $2.63$, and the force at $c\simeq-0.30$ and $2.00$.
