# On Tunnel Fusion and Yangian $R$-matrices

## authors:
#Dmitry_Galakhov

## summary:
XXX spin-chain Hamiltonians can be derived within the $R$-matrix formalism. For a simple Lie algebra $\mathfrak{g}$, the rational $R$-matrix $R$ intertwines the coproduct $Δ$ in the Yangian $\mathsf{Y}(\mathfrak{g})$. $R$ admits a particular Gauss decomposition $R=LDU$, where the upper-triangular matrix $U$ can be obtained from the lower-triangular matrix $L$. We treat $U$ in three ways. Algebraically, $U$ is known as a factorization twist. It plays the role of a ``universalizer'': it conjugates a non-universal pseudo-coproduct $\mathscr{D}$ in the molten crystal chain basis to the true Yangian coproduct $Δ=U^{-1}\mathscr{D} U$. Physically, $U$ can be obtained as a Berry phase in SUSY quiver QFT for a quiver associated with the Dynkin diagram of $\mathfrak{g}$. In this case, $U$ is calculated as a sum over tunneling amplitudes. Geometrically, $U$ can be interpreted as a renormalized Maulik-Okounkov stable envelope localized at a fixed point. In the physical setting, the Yang-Baxter equation (YBE), which ensures integrability, follows from the flatness of the Berry connection, which in turn follows from supersymmetry. However, the YBE imposes subtle constraints on the tunneling amplitudes, which we examine here. We construct recursion relations that allow higher collective amplitudes to be obtained from more elementary ones. We observe that different tunneling channels are in fact intertwined, leading to finer cancellations among amplitudes and to wall-crossing phenomena: depending on the parameters, the same amplitude is represented as a sum over different channels. We focus on $\mathsf{Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_2)$ with higher-spin and non-evaluation representations, as well as on the fundamental representation of $\mathsf{Y}(\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1})$.
