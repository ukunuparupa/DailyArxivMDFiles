# Going beyond the standard quasiparticle picture: Entanglement spreading after quenching from highly entangled states

## authors:
#Elisa_Sabadini
#Vincenzo_Alba

## summary:
The quasiparticle picture provides a quantitative description of entanglement spreading after quantum quenches in integrable systems. In interacting models, however, it relies on the assumption that the quench creates entangled pairs of quasiparticles, which is justified only for the so-called integrable initial states. Here we go beyond this setting by studying quenches in the XXZ spin chain from the $n$-rainbow states, a family of macroscopically homogeneous states formed by increasingly long-range Bell pairs, which are not integrable for $n>1$. The local stationary state after the quench approaches the infinite-temperature state as $n\to\infty$, with corrections of order $1/n$, as we show analytically. Nevertheless, in the noninteracting XX chain the quench produces entangled multiplets of $2n$ species of quasiparticles. Although interspecies correlations vanish as $1/n$, they strongly suppress the growth of the entanglement entropy, whose rate vanishes as $n\to\infty$. We capture this behaviour within a generalized quasiparticle picture. In the interacting XXZ chain, instead, time-dependent density matrix renormalization group (tDMRG) simulations show that the entanglement entropy grows at the rate at which thermodynamic entropy is exchanged across the cut. Within the time scales accessible to tDMRG, the data show no trace of the multiplet suppression. This holds both after homogeneous quenches and after joining the $n$-rainbow state with an infinite-temperature state, for which we conjecture a formula for the von Neumann entropy.
