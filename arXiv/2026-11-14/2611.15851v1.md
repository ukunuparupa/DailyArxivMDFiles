# Universal Theory of Strange and Bad Metals from Spatially Random Interactions with Underdamped Quantum-Critical Bosons

## authors:
#Aaron_Kleger
#Rufus_Boyack

## summary:
One of the most important challenges in condensed matter physics is understanding the macroscopic properties of strange metals, particularly why their resistivity remains linear in temperature over several decades. We propose that a (2+1)d theory of fermions coupled to underdamped quantum-critical bosons through a spatially disordered interaction provides this long-sought-after theoretical explanation. The key novelty of our work is to establish the appropriate separation of scales so that the bosons are underdamped. Using this framework, we obtain two distinct forms for the fermionic self-energy: a conformal-limit solution, $Σ\sim\sqrtω$, and a marginal Fermi-liquid solution, $Σ\simω\log(1/ω)$, corresponding to regimes above and below the Mott-Ioffe-Regel limit, respectively. These solutions produce Planckian scattering, exhibit exact $T$-linear resistivity, $ω/T$ scaling in the optical conductivity, as well as $\sim T\log(1/T)$ heat capacity at low temperatures. Moreover, our theory naturally unifies strange- and bad-metal phenomena within a single framework, suggesting that we are now approaching a new epoch in understanding the physics of strongly correlated matter.
