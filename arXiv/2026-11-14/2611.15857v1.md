# Crystalline Equivalence Principle for Anomalies

## authors:
#Ching-Yu_Yao
#Ryohei_Kobayashi

## summary:
We develop a microscopic characterization of crystalline symmetry anomalies in bosonic lattice systems in (1+1) and (2+1) dimensions with a combination of crystalline and finite unitary internal symmetry. We construct anomaly indices of symmetry operators and derive their cocycle conditions and coboundary ambiguities. For generalized crystalline symmetries combining permutations of onsite Hilbert spaces with quantum cellular automata, anomaly indices take values in $H^3(B\widetilde Γ,U(1)_r)$ and $H^4(B\widetilde Γ,U(1)_r)$ respectively, where $\widetilde Γ$ is the total group of crystalline and internal symmetries, and the coefficient twist $r$ accounts for spatial orientation reversal. When crystalline symmetries act solely by permuting onsite Hilbert spaces, the anomaly classes restrict trivially to the spatial subgroup. These results establish a crystalline equivalence principle for bosonic anomalies at the microscopic level, without assuming a continuum description or emergent Lorentz invariance. We prove that a nontrivial total anomaly index obstructs any symmetric invertible phase. The framework incorporates modulated symmetries where spatial symmetries permute internal symmetries. Our results reproduce LSM and SPT--LSM constraints, with the latter illustrating how spectral-sequence differentials can trivialize candidate LSM anomaly indices.
