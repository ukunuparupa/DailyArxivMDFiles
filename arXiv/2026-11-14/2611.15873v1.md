# Scalar and pseudoscalar glueballonia: do they bind?

## authors:
#Narmin_Nasibova
#Francesco_Giacosa

## summary:
We investigate the existence of molecular two-glueball bound states, so-called glueballonia, in pure Yang-Mills (YM) theory. We mainly concentrate on the scalar and pseudoscalar channels, thus studying scalar-scalar and scalar-pseudoscalar bound states, respectively. Near the onset of binding, when a bound state first appears just below threshold, a nonrelativistic framework is justified. We employ an attractive scalar-exchange Yukawa potential together with a smeared contact term contribution and determine the critical attraction required for binding. For two scalar glueballs, we use the scalar dilaton effective theory to determine the parameters of the non-relativistic potential: the Yukawa interaction is generated by t- and u-channels involving the exchange of one scalar glueball, while the repulsion emerges by the s-channel annihilation and contact interaction. Using lattice-YM input to fix the dilaton parameters, we find that the attraction is strong enough: the scalar glueballonium binds, and the result is quite robust with respect to parameter variations. In a minimal scalar-pseudoscalar extension, the Yukawa attraction is again given by the t-channel scalar glueball, while the crossed u-channel pseudoscalar exchange induces an attractive local contribution, making the pseudoscalar glueballonium also bind in pure gauge. Importantly, both the scalar and pseudoscalar glueballonia are stable in the YM vacuum. Next, we briefly comment on the pseudoscalar-pseudoscalar, scalar-tensor, and tensor-tensor channels: all these channels are attractive too and could form bound states, which, however, would be unstable in pure YM due to decay into two lighter glueballs. Finally, as a cross-check, our formalism confirms that Higgs-Higgs bound state does not form.
