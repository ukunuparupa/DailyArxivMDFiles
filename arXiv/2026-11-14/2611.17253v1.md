# Monopole catalysis with massive fermions: the fate of the twisted sector

## authors:
#Valentin_V_Khoze

## summary:
In the massless limit a fermion scattering on the minimal $SU(5)$ monopole in the lowest partial wave produces an outgoing state with half-integer fermion number in every flavour. This state lives in a twisted sector of the Hilbert space and is now understood as an ordinary fermion attached to a topological line running back to the monopole. In the Standard Model fermion masses and confinement destroy the line at distances greater than the inverse mass scales, causing the twisted state to decay into physical asymptotic states with integer fermion numbers. We study this decay for an incoming lepton or quark of one generation. The cross-section in this partial wave saturates the unitarity bound, $σ=π/p^2$, and the energy of the outgoing state is shared among the four fermion flavours with a broad Dirichlet distribution, which sets the thresholds and the kinematics. Conservation laws allow exactly two decay channels for each incoming fermion, the helicity flip and its $(B+L)$-violating partner. For an incoming electron the $(B+L)$-violating channel is anti-nucleon synthesis, and we determine the branching ratios in two limits: the two channels are equally likely at high energy, and only the helicity flip remains at low energy, below the anti-nucleon threshold. In between we model the energy dependence. For a quark in a slow proton the $(B+L)$-violating channel is catalysed proton decay, which has no threshold and occurs with a probability close to one. Callan's statistical interpretation of the fractional state emerges in a sharper form: the state is not a superposition of two final states with equal weights, but a single state that decays into them with energy-dependent probabilities, equal only at high energy.
