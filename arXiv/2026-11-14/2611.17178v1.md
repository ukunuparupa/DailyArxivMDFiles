# Covariant Wigner--Weyl calculus, non-dissipative transport and the chiral anomaly in inhomogeneous systems with nontrivial momentum-space topology

## authors:
#M_A_Zubkov

## summary:
We review, systematize and extend the covariant Wigner--Weyl calculus for fermions in non-Abelian gauge fields and its applications to systems inhomogeneous in coordinate space with nontrivial momentum-space topology: the non-Abelian chiral separation and quantum Hall effects, their stability under interactions, the Fermi-surface invariant of inhomogeneous systems and the non-elliptic models that carry it, with their equilibrium chiral magnetic effect, the factorization of the chiral anomaly into the instanton number and the momentum-space invariant $N_3$, and the noncommutative-geometric form of the invariants. Previous results are brought to common conventions, with hypotheses stated and notions defined. New results: the characterization of the operators whose covariant symbol is gauge-field independent and the ordering independence of the integrated anomaly; the anomaly factorization on a closed four-manifold without the homotopy hypothesis, the factorization into instanton number and $N_3$ holding exactly when the untwisted index vanishes; the Luttinger--Ward identity behind the renormalized-velocity current and the continuum conditions replacing the compactness of the Brillouin zone in the non-renormalization theorems; the identification of the Moyal $N_3$ of the chiral separation effect with the fibre degree of the principal symbol; the residue evaluation of the Fermi-surface invariant of the non-elliptic models, in the continuum and on a lattice, and the equilibrium current of the conserved charge along the magnetic field that it implies -- a chiral magnetic effect permitted by the non-Hamiltonian form of these models -- which the model with spherical Fermi surfaces trades for a chiral separation effect and a single-Weyl anomaly, and a modification of it restores at the price of the anomaly; and the index-pairing form of the chiral separation invariant.
