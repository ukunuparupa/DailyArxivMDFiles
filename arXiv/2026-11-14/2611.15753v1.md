# Radiative stability of species-separated Kaluza--Klein spectra in warped geometry

## authors:
#M_A_Anacleto
#F_A_Brito
#E_Passos
#Joao_R_L_Santos
#J_A_V_Campos

## summary:
Species-dependent fifth-direction lightcones, generated by scalar-gradient operators, can separate gauge and fermion Kaluza--Klein (KK) scales in a warped extra dimension, as recently shown by Randall and Riojas. In this work, we study whether this separation survives quantum corrections. Quark loops can only move the gluon speed within the interval spanned by the bare speeds. Gluon exchange pulls the quark speed toward the gluon speed, with a one-loop coefficient strongly enhanced by fifth-component exchange when the gluon is the slow species. We set the size of this effect using the strong-coupling estimates for a light-gluon benchmark, where a $5.5$~TeV gluon sits below quark partners at $10.74$~TeV. Within the effective theory, the Kaluza--Klein (KK) modes then correct the quark speed at order one. That correction is several times larger than the mass ordering can absorb, so the light-gluon ordering has no perturbative protection. The reverse benchmark, with slow quarks and a heavy gluon, keeps its ordering roughly ten times more robust.
