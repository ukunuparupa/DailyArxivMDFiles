# Matching lattice and continuum chiral determinants at finite volume

## authors:
#Zhengwen_Liu

## summary:
Recent formulations of lattice chiral gauge theories define a complex fermion weight either through Craig's gauge-invariant overlap measure or, following Hsu and Witten, through a continuum Weyl determinant on a reconstructed gauge connection, with its phase fixed by the five-dimensional $η$-invariant. For one $SU(5)$ generation on a flat four-torus, we prove that their relative weights agree in the continuum limit along smooth gapped paths, after fixing a normalization and subtracting a local Yang-Mills counterterm, whose coefficient we determine. On bounded smooth families a uniform Dirac gap suffices: the vacuum expansion need not converge. We obtain explicit uniform convergence rates for the logarithmic variations and, for Craig's interpolation, prove the reconstruction bounds that the matching requires, extending Hsu's small-field comparison to finite amplitude. For Hsu's reflection-positive reconstruction with flat collars, the phase variations still match, while the logarithmic coefficient of the quadratic counterterm is enhanced by a calculable, profile-dependent factor. In a hypercharge background on which the vacuum expansion diverges, numerical comparisons at the input gauge field support quadratic convergence of the phase, whereas the modulus acquires a logarithmic correction to quadratic scaling, whose coefficient follows analytically from the free overlap vacuum polarization.
