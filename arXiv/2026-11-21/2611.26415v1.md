# Half-strings across the deconfinement transition of a $U(1)$ quantum link model

## authors:
#Indrajit_Sau
#Arnab_Sen
#Debasish_Banerjee

## summary:
The phenomenon of confinement is arguably the key feature of gauge theories regularly studied in high-energy and condensed matter physics. This causes hadrons to exist as stable particles in quantum chromodynamics (QCD) as bound states of quarks (and gluons), and strings in pure gauge theories. The promise of realizing such strings in quantum simulator setups have fueled extensive studies of a plethora of simpler lattice gauge theories (LGTs). Quantum link models (QLMs) generalize Wilsonian LGTs and yield richer scenarios of confinement. For example, the string joining two static charges in the spin-$\frac{1}{2}$ $U(1)$ QLM fractionalize, giving rise to confining phases that break the point group and internal symmetries spontaneously. It was further shown in PRL 130, 071901 (2023) that adding annealed $Q = \pm 2$ charges to this model qualitatively changes the confinement-deconfinement transition. In this paper, we examine the finite temperature phase transition both in the presence and the absence of annealed charges through detailed extraction of critical temperature, $T_c$ , and exponents. The (effective) string tension $σ$ at zero and finite- temperatures are compared between the theory with and without annealed charges. The quantity $T_c/\sqrtσ$ for the QLM with annealed charges is similar to the usual $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge theory, while the theory without the charges provides a new universal value.
