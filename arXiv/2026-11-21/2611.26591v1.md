# Which black hole modes ring freely? Horizon regularity, the forced ringdown transient, and GW250114

## authors:
#Jahed_Abedi
#Niayesh_Afshordi

## summary:
The quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a black hole are defined by keeping only ingoing waves at the horizon. We ask what horizon regularity requires of the outgoing waves this condition discards. For a scalar-like perturbation, bounded first derivatives in a frame regular at the future horizon rule out outgoing waves that decay more slowly than the surface gravity $κ$, and smoothness singles out a ladder of frequencies, $ω=mΩ_{\rm H}-ijκ$ with $j=1,2,\dots$ and $Ω_{\rm H}$ the horizon angular velocity. We call these the horizon modes. For Schwarzschild black holes and the dominant $\ell=m=2$ Kerr modes, only the fundamental QNM decays more slowly than $κ$. These local statements leave the QNM spectrum unchanged but matter for merger ringdowns, because a source falling through the horizon drives the exterior at the horizon frequencies. Each QNM then has a driven and a free part, and the free part takes over only once the driving decays faster than the mode. The fundamental mode separates from the driving early. The overtones stay driven until the source is hidden behind the potential barrier, so overtone amplitudes fitted near the signal peak are in general not those of free oscillations. In GW250114, a ringdown model of the fundamental mode plus the first horizon mode, $ω=2Ω_{\rm H}-iκ$, is preferred over the fundamental mode plus its first overtone, with Bayes factors between 2 and 30, for start times from $2M_f$ before to $8M_f$ after coalescence. The horizon-mode fit returns a remnant that is heavier and spins faster than the inspiral predicts, as expected from nonlinear backreaction: the horizon that emits the mode has not yet settled to its final mass and spin. Zero-noise injections of numerical-relativity-calibrated waveforms reproduce both the preference and the shift, which are therefore features of general relativity.
