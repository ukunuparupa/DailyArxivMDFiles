# Boundary preparation and anomalous relaxation near a black hole exceptional point

## authors:
#Yu_Shi
#Sheng_Long

## summary:
We study the relaxation of a Robin scalar field near a stable exceptional point of a planar Schwarzschild AdS black hole, where finite boundary driving controls the recovery time without changing the leading decay rate at linear order. At the exceptional point, two weighted moments of the source determine the time dependence of the leading response. When the zeroth moment vanishes while the first remains nonzero, the linear time factor is eliminated, leaving an ordinary exponential decay. With suitable boundary preparation, a perturbation that retains more energy at release can nevertheless reach a common recovery threshold sooner. At the exceptional point, its recovery time advantage over a fixed reference that retains the linear factor grows as $γ_*^{-1}\log\log(1/θ)$ as the common amplitude threshold $θ$ approaches zero, where $γ_*$ is the common decay rate. The source moments also determine the constant term in this asymptotic time difference. Full scalar field evolution on a fixed background near the exceptional point illustrates the reversal for pulses matched in duration, boundary task, source cost, and injected work, within the time window where the small spectral splitting remains unresolved. The injected work also modifies the final spectrum through gravitational heating. When weak driving begins with a black hole at the exceptional point, the temperature rise is quadratic in the source amplitude, whereas the final pole splitting is linear in its magnitude. This splitting limits the time window over which the exceptional point recovery law can be observed.
