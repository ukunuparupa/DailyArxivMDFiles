# Light fermions can trigger a Brout-Englert-Higgs effect

## authors:
#Axel_Maas
#Sofie_Martins
#Georg_Wieland

## summary:
The Brout-Englert-Higgs effect produces the masses of the Standard Model particles. However, the cause of this effect is unknown. Beyond-the-Standard-Model theories aim to find the source of this effect, for example using a composite Higgs and dynamically generating its vacuum expectation value. In this paper, we show evidence from lattice simulations with vectorially coupled fermions and without Yukawa couplings that the Brout-Englert-Higgs effect could be triggered by the light masses of fermions. This suggests that an explanation entirely within the Standard Model could be possible. Moreover, our results are consistent with weak couplings and fermions can only impact the Higgs potential at NNLO. This implies a so far unidentified mechanism in an SU(2) scalar-fermion-gauge system that produces a non-zero vacuum expectation value. In addition, we find that the transition from a QCD-like behavior to a Higgs-like behavior happens when the fermion mass falls below twice the W/Z mass: a threshold that is integer valued but not connected to other critical physical behavior.
