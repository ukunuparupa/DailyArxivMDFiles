# Charge at regular centers in curvature-coupled electrodynamics

## authors:
#Ye_Zhou

## summary:
An electric or magnetic flux through shrinking symmetry spheres ordinarily produces an $r^{-4}$ Maxwell stress and a singular center. We examine how this obstruction changes when the Maxwell invariant is multiplied by an arbitrary scalar-curvature response $h(R)$. For real, static, spherically symmetric solutions, the difference between the radial and temporal gravitational equations gives a linear equation for a composite response variable. Combined with the trace equation, it bounds the response-weighted Maxwell source whenever the Kretschmann scalar is bounded, without assuming a limit or derivative bounds for the Ricci scalar. Magnetic and electric displacement charges cannot coexist, and neither charge is possible if $h$ remains bounded and bounded away from zero. On either surviving charged branch, the Ricci scalar converges and the weighted source tends to zero. Magnetic charge requires $h=o(r^4)$, whereas electric displacement requires $r^4|h|\to\infty$. Exact solutions show that these thresholds are sharp and realize the $r^6$ magnetic and $r^{-6}$ electric rates for a smooth geometric center. We also classify the zero-response sector, where an electric field may survive even though its displacement charge vanishes.
