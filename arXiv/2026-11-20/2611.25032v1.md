# Twist and Shout: defect lines on the Rényi torus

## authors:
#Naveen_Balaji_Umasankar

## summary:
We consider what the entanglement between two regions of a critical system can reveal about its symmetries, including non-invertible ones. For two intervals in a two-dimensional conformal field theory, the second Rényi entropy is a partition function on a torus, and a topological defect line can be wrapped around one of the intervals. For an on-site symmetry this computes the overlap of the reduced density matrix with its image under the symmetry acting on one interval, and in general it defines an observable that depends only on the cross-ratio. As the intervals approach each other, the observable vanishes at a rate set by the lightest operator on which the line can end, and its monodromy is fixed by the spins of such operators. Since these spins are constrained by the fusion category, the observable detects 't Hooft anomalies and distinguishes categories with the same fusion rules. It can be computed in closed form for a $\mathbb{Z}_2$, a $\mathbb{Z}_3$ and a Fibonacci line of the $(E_8)_1$ theory, and for the spin-flip and Kramers-Wannier lines of the Ising model, where the spin-flip result agrees with an exact computation in the critical Ising chain. Averaged over a symmetry, the observable reduces to a known Jones-index bound on the mutual information. We also show that the associated twist-field crossing equations are a form of the modular bootstrap, and discuss whether the full fusion category can be recovered from entanglement on surfaces of higher genus.
