# The BSM Rollercoaster, 1980-2000

## authors:
#Savas_Dimopoulos

## summary:
This contribution is a personal historical retrospective on beyond-the-Standard-Model (BSM) physics from 1978 to the early 2000s, organized around its emotional rollercoaster. I trace two waves of optimism separated by a multiyear contraction of confidence, both ultimately ending in disappointment when decisive experimental confirmation failed to arrive.  The story begins in 1978, when the Standard Model emerged as the front-runner. It then moves from the first wave of excitement over naturalness and Technicolor, rumored proton-decay events, and the construction of the Minimal Supersymmetric Standard Model, to the contraction of confidence in the late 1980s. A dramatic revival came in 1991, when the LEP and SLC precision data pointed toward supersymmetric unification, but it eventually gave way to renewed disappointment over the absence of superpartners and the sharpening of the LEP paradox.  I also describe how the realization that Newtonian gravity had been tested only down to distances of order centimeters opened the door to large extra dimensions. Finally, I discuss how Weinberg's 1987 anthropic argument for the cosmological constant, the 1998 discovery of cosmic acceleration, and the emergence of the string landscape changed the intellectual status of naturalness itself and led to Split Supersymmetry.  I have tried to describe not only what was proposed, but why these ideas felt compelling at the time, why confidence rose and fell, and how later evidence changed our sense of what could count as an explanation.
