# Information Geometry of DGLAP Evolution: From Geodesic Tests to Emergent Small-Circle Geometry

## authors:
#Wei_Kou
#Xurong_Chen

## summary:
We investigate the geometric structure of leading-order (LO)  nonsinglet DGLAP evolution on the Fisher--Rao manifold of  normalized valence distributions, asking whether the QCD-selected  trajectory is geodesic and how its departure from geodesic motion  is organized. Through the isometric embedding  $Φ=2\sqrt{p}$, the evolution is represented as a curve on  a Hilbert sphere of radius two, with its velocity and acceleration  determined directly by the DGLAP operator. For a GRV98-based  total-valence trajectory over  $2\leq Q^2\leq 2000~{\rm GeV}^2$, we find a nonzero but  weakly scale-dependent geodesic curvature. An arc-length-weighted  principal-component analysis shows that two affine directions  capture more than $99.999\%$ of the trajectory variance. The  corresponding affine plane intersects the Fisher sphere in a  small circle, whose predicted geodesic curvature closely agrees  with that reconstructed from the DGLAP-generated acceleration.  An $x$-resolved analysis further reveals that Fisher motion  is governed by relative PDF reshaping rather than probability  weight alone, with different momentum-fraction regions  contributing to motion along the trajectory and its transverse  bending. The Mellin representation provides a complementary  perspective on the effective dimensional reduction through the  collective evolution of the spectral hierarchy. These results  establish a quantitative information-geometric characterization  of the reference LO DGLAP trajectory and reveal an organized,  near-small-circle structure over the finite evolution interval.
