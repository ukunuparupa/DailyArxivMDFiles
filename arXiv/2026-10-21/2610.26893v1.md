# Aspects of Anomalies: Fermion Family Constraints, Gravitational Form Factors and Scattering Amplitudes

## authors:
#Dario_Melle

## summary:
Quantum anomalies play two complementary roles in quantum field theory: as consistency conditions that constrain the field content of a theory, and as dynamical effects encoded in the analytic structure of correlation functions. This thesis explores both. In the first part, we study the 331 model, in which gauge anomalies cancel across families rather than within each one. Combined with QCD asymptotic freedom, this fixes the number of fermion generations to three. We analyze the model's symmetry breaking, scalar and flavor sectors, and its TeV-scale Landau pole. We then study the collider phenomenology of its exotic quarks, showing that the pair production of heavy quarks decaying into doubly-charged vector bileptons gives a clean same-sign dimuon plus b-jets signature, observable at a 100 TeV collider but not at the LHC. Finally, we introduce the left-right symmetric 33311 extension, which preserves the family-number constraint and avoids proton decay. In the second part, we use conformal field theory in momentum space to study the chiral, gravitational and conformal anomalies. We construct a complete off-shell parametrization of the non-Abelian TJJ correlator in perturbative QCD, identifying the dilaton-like anomaly pole. For the AVV, ATT and TJJ vertices, we prove mass-independent anomaly sum rules for the spectral density, showing that the anomaly pole is not an asymptotic particle state. We apply the TJJ insertion to the pion gravitational form factors at large momentum transfer with Sudakov resummation: the anomaly feeds mainly into the D-term and the trace form factor, improving agreement with lattice QCD. Lastly, sewing two anomaly vertices through graviton exchange, we show that the trace anomaly activates a gauge-invariant scalar gravitational channel, giving a double-copy-like structure in photon and graviton scattering.
