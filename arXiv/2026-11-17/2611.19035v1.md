# A Bound on Wormhole Probing Time from Holographic Multi-Entropy

## authors:
#Takanori_Anegawa
#Norihiro_Iizuka

## summary:
Bipartite entanglement entropy eventually saturates and therefore cannot track the continued growth of an eternal wormhole. Recently, it was shown that genuine tripartite multi-entropy can probe wormhole growth even after bipartite entanglement entropies have saturated. This naturally raises the question: can increasing the number of parties extend the probing time indefinitely? Adopting the multiway maximin prescription and assuming that its selected representatives are regular spacetime-stationary networks, we prove that the answer is no for nonrotating BTZ black holes. We establish a common finite upper bound on the probing time for every fixed finite boundary partition, independent of the number, sizes, and arrangement of its parties. For any network on any Cauchy slice, there exists a comparison network that assigns the entire central region containing the wormhole interior to a single party. A network longer than this comparison network cannot minimize length on that slice. The central length required by a stationary network connecting the two boundaries grows with time, whereas the central length of the comparison network remains uniformly bounded. At sufficiently late times, such a network therefore cannot minimize length on any Cauchy slice containing it and is excluded from the maximin prescription. Consequently, beyond this bound, no network selected by maximin enters the black-hole interior.
