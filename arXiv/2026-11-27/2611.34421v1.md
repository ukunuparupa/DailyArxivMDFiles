# Quark-Loop Corrections and Auxiliary Field-Strength Mixing in First-Order QCD

## authors:
#Hasnat_Mohammad_Nayeem

## summary:
In the first-order formulation of Yang-Mills theory the gluon field strength is an independent auxiliary field $F$, and the only gluon self-interaction is a momentum-independent cubic vertex. We determine how quarks, which couple only to the gauge field, act on this structure. Their divergences are multiples of $f(A)\cdot f(A)$, a term absent from the first-order Lagrangian that no rescaling can produce. Working with the background-field method in a general covariant gauge, we find that the quark sector is renormalized entirely by the mixing of the auxiliary field with the field strength known from pure Yang-Mills theory, $F_0=F+z\,f(A)$, with quark coefficient $z=-α_sT_FN_f/(6π\barε)$, together with a rescaling of the gluon field and no rescaling of $F$, so that the renormalized auxiliary field runs into the gluon field strength. When a heavy quark of mass $M$ is integrated out, the same mixing survives as a finite threshold coefficient $c=(α_sT_F/6π)\ln(M^2/μ^2)$, whose scale dependence is exactly the heavy-quark share of the running, and the invariant combination of the couplings reproduces the decoupling of $α_s$. Below the threshold, when the leading power correction is kept as a gauge-sector operator, the auxiliary field is no longer the field strength: $F=f(A)+(gα_sT_F/15πM^2)(D_αJ_β-D_βJ_α)$, with $J$ the light-quark colour current. We verify this identity against the full theory and show that it separates the long-distance gluon exchange, carried by $F$, from the short-distance heavy-quark effect, carried by a four-quark contact term.
