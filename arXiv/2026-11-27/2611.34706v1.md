# Entanglement and HBAR entropy in a Lorentzian non-commutative black hole

## summary:
We investigate how Lorentzian non-commutative smearing affects entanglement and horizon brightened acceleration radiation (HBAR) outside a black hole. For a massless scalar field, tracing over the interior partner gives the exterior quantum channel, its logarithmic negativity, mutual information, and Bell entanglement fidelity. At fixed mass and Killing frequency, increasing the smearing lowers the surface gravity, increases the accessible correlations, and suppresses the leading atomic emission probability. The horizon displacement begins at order $\sqrtΘ$, while the thermal correction at infinity begins at order $Θ$. At fixed local frequency, the redshift introduces a term of order $\sqrtΘ$ at fixed areal radius. Holding the proper distance fixed, it cancels this term and recovers the common Rindler response close to the horizon. For a freely falling atom, emission and absorption obey detailed balance at the Hawking temperature in the leading horizon approximation. Random atomic injection drives the selected mode from the vacuum to a thermal state with an exact relaxation law and nonnegative entropy production. Near equilibrium, we obtain $\dot S_{\rm HBAR}\simeq\dot A_p/4$ when the radiated energy is drawn from horizon heat, where $\dot A_p$ is the positive radiative area loss. When the radiation is assigned to ADM mass loss at fixed smearing length $\ell=4\sqrt{Θ/π}$, the result is $\dot S_{\rm HBAR}\simeq\dot A_p/[4(1-\ell/r_+)]$. The mass dependence of the exterior source thus enters the entropy per unit area lost at order $\sqrtΘ$.
