# Distance-Higuchi Bounds on Inflationary Field Ranges and Lifetimes

## authors:
#Luis_A_Anchordoqui
#Ignatios_Antoniadis
#Alek_Bedroya

## summary:
We show that quasi-de Sitter inflation driven by a nearly flat scalar potential has a finite polynomial lifespan dictated by the interplay between the swampland Distance Conjecture and the generalized Higuchi bound. By analyzing classical scalar rolling and quantum stochastic diffusion, we demonstrate that for any arbitrarily high but fixed statistical confidence, a universe cannot live longer than $\sim \min\left\{\ln(\frac{1}{H})\frac{\sqrt V}{V'},V^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}\ln^2(\frac{1}{H})\right\}$ in reduced Planck units while remaining Higuchi-consistent, where $H$ is the Hubble parameter, $V$ is the scalar potential, $d$ is the number of spacetime dimensions, and prime denotes derivative with respect to the scalar field. This bound despite being weaker than the Trans-Planckian Censorship Conjecture, which has been argued for classical cosmologies that flow to the asymptotic of the field space without tunneling, is powerful given its minimal quantum gravity input and applicability to all points in the moduli space.
