# From Quasinormal Mode Poles to Branch Cuts at Finite $N$

## authors:
#Navid_Abbasi

## summary:
In the strict large-$N$ limit, holographic thermal retarded correlators are typigcally meromorphic, with their late-time behavior determined by isolated quasinormal mode (QNM) poles. We show that the first bulk-loop correction, of order $1/N^2$, qualitatively changes this structure. In a scalar theory on the planar AdS$_5$ black brane, a one-loop bulk diagram generates poles at sums of two QNM frequencies at fixed loop momentum $q$; integration over the continuous momentum turns these QNM-pair poles into branch cuts. At vanishing external spatial momentum, for pairs built from QNM branches with opposite real parts, the leading light-cone contributions cancel at large momentum, and the residual QNM-pair frequency scales as $q^{-1/3}$. Consequently, an infinite family of cuts accumulates at zero frequency, while all tree-level QNM poles remain at finite nonzero frequencies. The first finite-$N$ correction therefore closes the gap set by the classical QNM spectrum. Competition between the decreasing QNM-pair damping and thermal Boltzmann suppression produces a moving saddle and a finite-$N$ contribution $G(t)\sim N^{-2}t^αe^{-Ct^{3/4}},\, C>0,$ which decays more slowly than the leading classical QNM contribution. Thus the large-$N$ expansion is nonuniform at late times, and bulk quantum fluctuations generate a distinct late-time regime beyond the classical QNM decay.
