# Astrophysical Constraints for Bosonic Dark Matter in and Around Quark Stars

## authors:
#Júlia_Tartera_Barberà
#Sarah_Louisa_Pitz
#Jürgen_Schaffner-Bielich
#Laura_Tolos

## summary:
We investigate the structure, stability, and mass-radius relations of compact stars composed of quark matter within the non-ideal MIT Bag Model mixed with self-interacting bosonic dark matter with a $φ^n$ self-interaction potential. By solving the coupled two-fluid Tolman-Oppenheimer-Volkoff equations for boson masses between 200 MeV and 1 GeV and self-interaction strengths with $n=4$ and $n=40$, we show that strong self-interactions and higher boson masses enhance the stability of compact star configurations at high central pressures, though they reduce maximum overall masses. Ultra-compact solutions with $C \ge 1/3$ capable of mimicking black holes are significantly restricted compared to hadronic models, emerging only for strong self-interactions and dark matter fractions exceeding 90\%. Depending on the parameters, the compact stars form either extended dark matter halos or compact cores: stronger self-interactions and higher boson masses suppress the radial extension to favor compact cores of dark matter. Matching our models against observational constraints highlights PSR J1614$-$2230 and XTE J1814$-$338 as potential window of opportunity for dark matter detection. Notably, XTE J1814$-$338 could confirm a dark sector, given its requirement for light boson masses ($m_b \approx 200\text{--}300\text{ MeV}$) and strongly DM-dominated configurations.
