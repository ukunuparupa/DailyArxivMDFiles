# Algebraic instantons and non-Gaussian vacuum in quantum mechanics

## authors:
#Rahim_Aziz
#Hao_Geng
#Thomas_Schaefer
#Mithat_Ünsal

## summary:
The standard instanton calculus in quantum mechanics is based on harmonic minima in the potential. When the perturbative vacuum is anharmonic, $V\simeqλx^{2n}$ with $n\ge2$, the standard construction fails. We study this problem for a particle on a circle and subject to a potential $V(x)=16λ\sin^4(x/2)$. The instanton and its fluctuation operator are known in closed form, but they differ qualitatively from the familiar ones. Because $V''=0$ at the minimum, the classical vacuum is gapless, the instanton decays algebraically (an algebraic instanton), the zero mode is located at the threshold of a continuous spectrum, and the one-loop determinant is infrared divergent. Quantum mechanics generates a non-perturbative gap $E_q\propto\hbar^{4/3}λ^{1/3}$, and the instanton is embedded in a vacuum that is dynamically gapped, similar to what happens in Yang--Mills theory. We show that the one-instanton amplitude can be computed by matching the instanton trajectory to the exact vacuum part of the path integral. The resulting prefactor scales as $\hbar^{4/3}$, and this expression agrees with the numerical spectrum to a very high accuracy. The classical interaction between instantons decays as $T^{-3}$, whereas the correct result is an exponential decay controlled by the dynamically generated gap, beyond the quantum time scale. We provide an alternative derivation of this result based on a sum over intermediate states.
