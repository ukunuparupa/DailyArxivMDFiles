# Simple Models of Thermal Order in $2+1$ Dimensions

## authors:
#Michael_Smolkin
#Lev_Yung

## summary:
Heating need not destroy order. We show that it persists to arbitrarily high temperatures in simple quantum field theories in $2+1$ spacetime dimensions. In a $\mathbb Z_2\times\mathbb Z_2$ model of two real scalar fields, a sufficiently negative mixed quartic coupling drives one field to condense, spontaneously breaking its $\mathbb Z_2$ symmetry. When coupled to massless Dirac fermions through a Yukawa interaction, this condensate generates a fermion mass, thereby spontaneously breaking parity and time-reversal symmetries. We extend this construction to a $\mathbb Z_2\times O(n)$ scalar model, in which the Mermin-Wagner theorem precludes spontaneous breaking of the continuous $O(n)$ symmetry at finite temperatures. Nevertheless, for $n=2$, a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless superfluid phase persists to arbitrarily high temperatures. In this phase, spatial correlations decay algebraically, with an exponent that approaches zero as the temperature increases. For $n\geq 3$, we uncover a further unexpected feature of these models: the characteristic size of ordered domains grows asymptotically with temperature, revealing an unusual enhancement of spatial correlations upon heating. We establish our results using a controlled high-temperature expansion, with selected findings corroborated by numerical functional renormalization group analysis. The simplicity of the field content in these models provides a promising starting point for exploring experimental realizations of persistent thermal order in thin-film materials.
