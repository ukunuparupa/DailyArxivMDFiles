# Torsional Hall Viscosity of Massive Chern Insulators: Finite Temperature and Chemical Potential

## summary:
We study the torsional Hall viscosity $ζ_{\rm H}$ of Chern insulators at finite temperature and chemical potential. Our analysis considers the massive Dirac fermion and two deformations: a constant out-of-plane magnetic field and a momentum-dependent mass described by the single-block Bernevig-Hughes-Zhang (BHZ) Hamiltonian. For the massive Dirac theory, we show that temperature and chemical potential lead to UV-finite corrections and that a chemical potential outside the bulk gap modifies $ζ_{\rm H}$ even at zero temperature. In a constant magnetic field, the response of massive Dirac fermions is dictated by the relativistic Landau-level spectrum, leading to a plateau structure at zero temperature that is smoothed at finite temperature. The jumps are proportional to the Dirac mass and inversely proportional to the squared magnetic length and the newly occupied Landau-level energy. Finally, we present a detailed study of the correction to $ζ_{\rm H}$ in the single-block BHZ continuum model due to temperature and chemical potential. For parameters relevant to (Hg,Mn)Te quantum wells, the magnitude of the jump across a mass domain wall retains its vacuum enhancement due to the BHZ deformation as long as the chemical potential lies sufficiently deep inside the gap compared with the thermal energy. Our results extend the theory of torsional Hall viscosity to experimentally relevant conditions and may facilitate its future detection.
