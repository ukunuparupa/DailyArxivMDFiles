# 'Forward-backward' flows and integration cycles for semiclassical Euclidean gravity

## authors:
#Nicholas_M_K_Ross
#Toby_Wiseman

## summary:
Integrating Euclidean semiclassical gravity over real Riemannian metrics is subtle due to the conformal factor problem. A perturbative prescription was given by Gibbons-Hawking-Perry (GHP) where the integration contour for the conformal perturbations is rotated to be imaginary. A potential non-perturbative remedy is that the integration cycles associated to semiclassical saddle points should be deformed into the space of complex metrics in the sense of Picard-Lefschetz theory. Here we propose a non-perturbative construction of such complex integration cycles. This employs a 'forward-backward' gradient flow of the Einstein-Hilbert action, starting at a saddle point, along which the action has monotonically increasing real part and constant imaginary part. The flow is not parabolic. In a neighbourhood of a saddle point, the real part of the conformal factor flows forward and that of the conformal class backwards, with the opposite true of their imaginary parts. Starting the flow at a saddle point, then roughly speaking, one specifies the real part of the conformal class and the imaginary part of the conformal factor at the end of the flow. Solving the flow one finds the complex metrics along a one parameter path within the integration cycle of that saddle, and varying this final data generates the integration cycle, the Lefschetz thimble of the saddle point. We find that near to a real saddle point this reproduces the GHP prescription. We characterise the neighbourhood of a saddle where such forward-backward flows may exist, finding a weaker restriction than that of Kontsevich-Segal-Witten. We numerically demonstrate flows explicitly for 4-dimensional gravity, constructing sections through the integration contours for the saddles given by Euclidean de Sitter, and for Schwarzschild in a cavity.
