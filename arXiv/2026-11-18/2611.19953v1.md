# Neumann zero modes and the locality of the derivative expansion

## authors:
#C_D_Fosco
#F_D_Mazzitelli

## summary:
The derivative expansion (DE) provides a useful approximation to the Casimir interaction energy between two close surfaces. It has the form of a local functional, obtained from a zero momentum expansion. However, when the system involves a massless field satisfying Neumann conditions, that expansion may develop nonanalytic terms. We present here a study on how this property reflects itself in the need to add to the DE some novel, nonlocal terms. These are, we argue, akin to the ones introduced in effective theories because of the presence of massless particles in loops. We pinpoint the mode responsible for this effect, and construct the effective action due to its fluctuations, showing that it reproduces known nonanalytic contributions, and present the corresponding nonlocal addition to the DE. We also predict a new nonanalyticity: Neumann conditions in $3+1$ dimensions at zero temperature produce a term of order $|{\mathbf k}_\shortparallel |^3$, with a parameter-free coefficient, between the (local) second and fourth orders of the expansion. We show that, for a cylinder in front of a plane, the zero-mode loop adds to the local expansion a term of relative order $(L/R)^{3/2}$, with a coefficient that we compute explicitly, showing that it accounts for the behaviour of the existing numerical fits for that geometry.
