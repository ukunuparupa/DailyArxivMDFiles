# Near-boundary and near-horizon momentum--Krylov response in noncommutative-inspired BTZ spacetime

## authors:
#F_A_Brito
#F_G_Costa
#J_A_V_Campos

## summary:
We compute the proper radial momentum of a massive probe falling into a Lorentzian-smeared, noncommutative-inspired BTZ black hole at fixed asymptotic mass, and use it as the growth rate of momentum--Krylov complexity. In the BTZ limit the accumulated complexity is $\mathcal{K}_0 = X(\coshτ- 1)$. With smearing, the initial growth coefficient is $F'_α(X)/4$, while the late-time exponent is the surface gravity $F'_α(x_+)/2$. The two depend differently on the smearing parameter, so the deformed response is not a rescaled BTZ curve; we show this analytically and confirm it numerically. For a distant release point the early response approaches BTZ, whereas the late-time rate does not. A BTZ black hole with the same Hawking temperature reproduces the response to within about ten percent, and the remaining difference is set by the early/late mismatch. A Gaussian mass profile gives the same picture. The horizon radius and the surface gravity vanish at the critical smearing with exponents $1/2$ and $3/2$. At fixed mass the boundary stress tensor coincides with that of BTZ, but the $z^3$ Fefferman--Graham coefficient and the lengths of boundary-anchored geodesics do not.
