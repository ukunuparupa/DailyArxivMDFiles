# The Hidden Simplicity of the Classical Limit

## authors:
#Yilber_Fabian_Bautista
#Stefano_De_Angelis
#David_A_Kosower
#Donal_O_Connell
#Matteo_Sergola

## summary:
We study the classical limit of two-to-two massive-scalar amplitudes at all orders of perturbation theory. We compute these amplitudes in the probe limit for several theories: a family of scalar theories, electrodynamics, and $\mathcal{N}=8$ supergravity with half-BPS matter. We show that related multiple-emission classical amplitudes obey a simple factorization derivable from the usual factorization of quantum amplitudes. The classical factorization, which reflects the second-order nature of classical dynamics, holds in more general theories --- including gravity. We further show that both the multiple-emission and the two-to-two classical amplitudes have a simple organization in terms of weighted trees. This allows a recursive computation to all orders in the probe limit. The probe's trajectory emerges as a generating function of trees. The required boundary conditions are given by the Feynman $i \varepsilon$ prescription, leading to a novel worldline Green's function. In our approach, the rich classical dynamics arises from simple combinatorics.
