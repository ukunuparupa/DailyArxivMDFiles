# Constraints on ultralight dark matter couplings from orbital perturbations of binary pulsars

## authors:
#Federico_Huxhagen
#Diana_López_Nacir

## summary:
Ultralight dark matter coupled directly to ordinary matter perturbs the orbits of binary pulsars, and the perturbation grows secularly when its frequency matches a harmonic of the orbital motion. We use this effect to constrain three couplings with the wideband timing data for the $50$ binary pulsars of the NANOGrav 15 yr data set: a linear and a quadratic universal coupling of a scalar field to matter, and a vector field coupled to baryon minus lepton number ($B-L$). For every system and for masses between $10^{-23}$ and $10^{-18}\,$eV, we build the timing signature from the perturbed orbital elements. We then sample the coupling together with the local amplitude, phase and direction of the field and the red noise of the pulsar, marginalizing analytically over the timing model, and combine the single-pulsar likelihoods into $95\%$ credible upper limits at each mass. For a universal linear coupling, the orbital resonances of the eccentric systems bring the limit close to the Cassini bound. For the quadratic coupling, treating the neutron star as a point particle, the limits are the strongest available between $2\times10^{-22}$ and $2\times10^{-21}\,$eV. In the case of vector ultralight dark matter, the limit is comparable to the PPTA and Eöt-Wash bounds between $3\times10^{-22}$ and $10^{-21}\,$eV and reaches or beats the Eöt-Wash bound at isolated orbital resonances, while MICROSCOPE remains stronger over the entire mass range.
