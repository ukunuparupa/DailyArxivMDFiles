# The Relaxation of Quantum Near-Extremal Black Holes. (I) Microcanonical States, Pruning of Quasinormal Modes, and the Aretakis Instability

## authors:
#Roberto_Emparan
#Sameer_Murthy
#Stefano_Trezzi

## summary:
The late-time relaxation of a near-extremal black hole is classically governed by an infinite tower of purely damped quasinormal modes. This poses a puzzle: the higher overtones decay at rates much larger than the energy $E$ of the black hole above extremality, so they would need black hole states below the extremal mass. We resolve it by including the quantum fluctuations that become large when $E$ falls below a scale $E_b$. We compute the retarded Green's function of a probe of conformal dimension $Δ$ in a black hole of fixed energy, and find that each classical mode splits into a pair of poles with real parts set by $E_b$. The perturbed black hole decays as an unstable quantum system, and a pair is a resonance, i.e., a quasinormal mode, only if its energy lies within the accessible black hole spectrum. This prunes the tower to finitely many modes, all damped faster than $Δ^2E_b$, and below $E=Δ^2E_b/2$ none is left. For generic $Δ$, the response of the black hole decays first as $t^{-2Δ}$ and finally as $t^{-3/2}$, with an exponential stage in between if $E\ggΔ^2E_b$.  The same quantum effects bear on the Aretakis instability of extremal black holes, in which transverse derivatives of a perturbation grow without bound on the horizon. Like the tower of modes, it originates in the scale invariance of the classical throat, which the quantum fluctuations break. The $t^{-2Δ}$ decay, which is the boundary counterpart of the Aretakis growth, ends by $t\sim1/E_b$, when the throat ceases to be classical, and by then horizon derivatives have grown only to a finite value. Their unbounded growth is an artifact of taking the semiclassical limit before the late-time one, two limits that in quantum mechanics do not commute.
