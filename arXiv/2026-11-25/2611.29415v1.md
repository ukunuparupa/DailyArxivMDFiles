# The Generalized Second Law from Fluctuation Theorems in a Quantum Reference Frame

## authors:
#Feng-Li_Lin
#Sayid_Mondal

## summary:
We prove the generalized second law (GSL) for black-hole exteriors undergoing unitary driving and relaxation on a fixed semifinite algebra. Wall's influential proof assumes semiclassical geometry and restricts fixed parent states to algebras of successive horizon cuts. We instead evolve the state on a fixed dressed exterior algebra. With complete characteristic data, the same semiclassical exterior supports both descriptions, analogous to the Heisenberg and Schrödinger pictures through state--operator duality. Using a gravitational charge as a quantum clock, we construct a modular crossed product with density operators whose trace entropy reproduces generalized entropy up to clock contributions. Our approach parallels thermodynamics in regulated field theory and formulates the GSL through a quantum channel fluctuation theorem (FT) for the open exterior. We develop three complementary formulations under specified dynamical assumptions. Separated unitary driving and relaxation combine modular work, the physical-process first law relating horizon absorption to area gain, and relative-entropy contraction through data processing. A time-local description accommodates simultaneous unitary driving and dissipation. A two-time surprisal FT treats the complete channel, identifies the nonunital correction, and yields a GSL bound for unital dressed evolution without resolving intermediate dynamics. Relaxation that does not mix distinct dressed-charge sectors and preserves the full dressed reference state is unital. Unitality is sufficient for this FT proof but is not necessary for the GSL along a particular evolution. The entropy bounds establish the GSL in a controlled slow-clock limit and quantify finite-width clock corrections.
